# Methods of Persuasion

This guide interprets “Methods of Persuasion 1–7” as Robert Cialdini’s seven principles of persuasion. The examples are original applications to branding and website design.

## 1. Reciprocity

**Definition:** People tend to want to return a favor after receiving something valuable.

**Design example:** A cooking website offers a free meal-planning worksheet. Visitors may then be more willing to subscribe to its recipe newsletter.

## 2. Scarcity

**Definition:** People often value an opportunity more when its availability is limited.

**Design example:** A pottery shop displays the actual number of handmade mugs remaining beside the purchase button, helping customers understand availability.

## 3. Authority

**Definition:** People are more likely to follow guidance from credible, knowledgeable experts.

**Design example:** An architecture firm's website places its architects' relevant qualifications and completed projects near its consultation form.

## 4. Commitment and Consistency

**Definition:** People tend to act consistently with commitments they have already made, especially voluntary ones.

**Design example:** A language-learning website invites visitors to choose a daily study goal, then offers a practice schedule that supports their choice.

## 5. Liking

**Definition:** People are more receptive to requests from people they like or relate to.

**Design example:** A neighborhood bakery uses friendly copy, staff photographs, and an owner story to help visitors connect with the people behind the business.

## 6. Social Proof (Consensus)

**Definition:** People look to others' behavior for guidance, particularly when they are uncertain.

**Design example:** A tutoring website shows authentic student reviews beside its booking options so prospective students can learn from others' experiences.

## 7. Unity

**Definition:** A shared group identity can make people more receptive to one another's influence.

**Design example:** A university alumni website highlights shared campus traditions and alumni stories alongside an invitation to join a mentoring program.

## Source

Definitions summarized from [Robert Cialdini's Seven Principles of Persuasion — Influence at Work](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/).
