# New Wave Typography

[Home](../README.md)

## What is it?

New Wave typography describes experimentation that challenged strict readings of Swiss typographic order. Wolfgang Weingart's work is an important reference: he used knowledge of printing to explore layering, letter spacing, and disrupted hierarchy. April Greiman's digital experiments show how expressive composition could also develop through new technology. The label describes a related field of experimentation, not identical methods or a shared manifesto. [Historical context](https://collections.lacma.org/object/207622).

## How do you recognize or use it?

- Type becomes expressive visual material as well as readable language.
- Layered images and patterns can create depth on a flat page.
- Changes in spacing, scale, and direction interrupt a regular rhythm.
- The printing or digital process may remain visible instead of being concealed.

**Suggested application:** For a hypothetical design conference, create an expressive title treatment and a clear supporting schedule. Make experimentation part of the subject while preserving the reader's ability to book or find a session. This distinguishes purposeful complexity from accidental confusion.

### Example 1: Kunstkredit Basel 1976/77

- **Creator:** Wolfgang Weingart.
- **Title:** *Kunstkredit Basel 1976/77*.
- **Date:** 1977.
- **Institution:** Los Angeles County Museum of Art (LACMA).
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://collections.lacma.org/object/207622).

**What it demonstrates:** Text, patterns, and photographic fragments overlap to complicate the picture plane. The poster demonstrates deliberate departure from a uniform typographic surface. Its detail depends on knowledge of printing, so the effect should not be mistaken for a careless layout.

**Image credit and reuse:** LACMA, M.2015.102.6. Gift of Allison and Larry Berg and Mark and Maura Resnick through the 2015 Decorative Arts and Design Acquisition Committee. Photo © Museum Associates/LACMA.

### Example 2: DOES IT MAKE SENSE? Design Quarterly #133

- **Creator:** April Greiman.
- **Title:** *DOES IT MAKE SENSE? Design Quarterly #133*.
- **Date:** 1986.
- **Institution:** MoMA, New York; originally made for Design Quarterly.
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://www.moma.org/audio/playlist/297/4835).

**What it demonstrates:** A magazine unfolds into a large composition combining a digitized self-portrait, text, and imagery. In MoMA's audio account, Greiman describes using video and a Macintosh to create the work. It demonstrates digital tools becoming part of an experimental visual language.

**Image credit and reuse:** MoMA audio/image record; collection credit: Gift of the designer. Artwork by April Greiman. Consult the museum and artist for image reproduction; no image is copied here.
