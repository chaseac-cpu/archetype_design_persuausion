# Memphis

[Home](../README.md)

## What is it?

Memphis was a design group established in Milan in 1981 around Ettore Sottsass and collaborators. Its objects challenged the expectation that good design should be visually restrained. Color, pattern, unconventional combinations, and theatrical form became central to the experience of furniture and lighting. Memphis is a particular group, not another name for every colorful postmodern design. [Historical context](https://www.vam.ac.uk/info/collection-selection-boxes-postmodern-design).

## How do you recognize or use it?

- Unexpected combinations of geometry make ordinary objects visually assertive.
- Surface pattern can be a major element rather than a minor finishing detail.
- Contrasting colors and materials can create a playful or deliberately awkward effect.
- Furniture may also function as a sculptural presence in a room.

**Suggested application:** For a hypothetical art-festival website, develop a small set of patterned shapes and colorful accents. Use them to establish a celebratory tone while keeping ticket details and forms on quiet backgrounds. This is a contemporary graphic adaptation of ideas from object design.

## Examples

These are historical works. Open each institutional link to view the work. Images are linked rather than reproduced because an open reuse license has not been established. The brief design readings explain why the examples are relevant; suggested website applications above are original interpretations.

### Example 1: 'Carlton' Room Divider

- **Creator:** Ettore Sottsass.
- **Title:** *'Carlton' Room Divider*.
- **Date:** 1981.
- **Institution:** MoMA, New York.
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/292736).

**What it demonstrates:** Angled and horizontal members make storage into a conspicuous composition. Plastic laminate and wood combine practical construction with a strong surface identity. The work helps explain why Memphis furniture can be experienced as an object to look at as well as use.

**Image credit and reuse:** MoMA, object 1290.2018; Gift of the George R. Kravis II Collection. Consult MoMA's image licensing guidance.

### Example 2: Super Lamp

- **Creator:** Martine Bedin; manufactured by Memphis/Milano.
- **Title:** *Super Lamp*.
- **Date:** Designed 1978; this example made ca. 1981–1988, according to MFAH.
- **Institution:** Museum of Fine Arts, Houston (MFAH).
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://emuseum.mfah.org/objects/145708/visit).

**What it demonstrates:** The wheeled body and exposed bulbs make a lamp feel like a playful companion. It offers a different expression of Memphis than Carlton while similarly making personality part of function. The record distinguishes the earlier design date from later Memphis manufacture.

**Image credit and reuse:** MFAH, object 2020.71; Museum purchase funded by the Design Council, 2020. Record image notice: © 1978 Martine Bedin. Image linked, not reproduced.
