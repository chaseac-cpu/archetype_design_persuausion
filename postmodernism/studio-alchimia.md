# Studio Alchimia and Redesign

[Home](../README.md)

## What is it?

Studio Alchimia was founded in Milan in 1976. Its designers explored alternatives to design understood mainly as industrial production. This page focuses on redesign: transforming familiar or historical forms through decoration, quotation, and new combinations. These practices helped make ornament and cultural reference central to postmodern Italian design. [Historical context](https://www.broehan-museum.de/en/exhibition/alchimia-the-revolution-of-italian-design/).

## How do you recognize or use it?

- Existing furniture forms can become the starting point for a new statement.
- Pattern and painting may compete with or transform the underlying structure.
- References to art and earlier periods produce several possible associations.
- An object can be expressive or theatrical as well as useful.

**Suggested application:** For an original exhibition website, combine a recognizable editorial layout with a clearly motivated visual quotation, such as a patterned border referencing the exhibition's subject. Explain the reference. Adding arbitrary decoration without a relationship to the content misses the point of redesign.

### Example 1: Poltrona di Proust armchair

- **Creator:** Alessandro Mendini.
- **Title:** *Poltrona di Proust armchair*.
- **Date:** 1978.
- **Institution:** MoMA, New York.
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/206491).

**What it demonstrates:** An ornate chair form is transformed by an all-over painted pattern. The combination makes surface and historical reference active parts of the design. It is a useful contrast with the exposed structural simplicity of Breuer's B3 chair.

**Image credit and reuse:** Collection and image source: MoMA. The linked object record provides licensing information; no unrestricted reproduction permission is assumed.

### Example 2: Kandissi sofa

- **Creator:** Alessandro Mendini.
- **Title:** *Kandissi sofa*.
- **Date:** 1978, following Mart's exhibition record.
- **Institution:** Mart, Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art of Trento and Rovereto (exhibition documentation); another example documented by Groninger Museum.
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://www.mart.tn.it/mostre/mendini-depero-138681).

**What it demonstrates:** The sofa combines a historical furniture silhouette with conspicuous decorative additions and patterned upholstery. Groninger Museum explains its references to Biedermeier furniture and Kandinsky. It shows redesign through overlapping references rather than the invention of a supposedly neutral form.

**Image credit and reuse:** Images linked from Mart's Mendini > Depero exhibition archive; no object-photo license is stated here. Mart is the documenting institution, not asserted to own the illustrated sofa. Groninger Museum provides a separate collection record.
