# Postmodern Historicist Architecture

[Home](../README.md)

## What is it?

Historicist postmodern architecture reintroduces recognizable references, ornament, and ambiguity into modern building design. Its architects could quote traditional forms without reproducing an entire historical building. Robert Venturi's work is a key early reference. This category is one strand of postmodernism; it does not describe every building produced after modernism. [Historical context](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/990).

## How do you recognize or use it?

- Gables, arches, columns, or other familiar forms can be quoted or exaggerated.
- Scale and symmetry may be manipulated to create tension.
- A facade can communicate through symbols as well as express construction.
- Historical reference and contemporary building needs can coexist.

**Suggested application:** For an original theater website, a simplified arch or ticket motif might signal a cultural setting while the page uses current interaction conventions. Choose references the audience can recognize. The visual analogy should support the subject, not imply a fabricated institutional history.

### Example 1: Vanna Venturi House, Scheme VI (final), scale model

- **Creator:** Robert Venturi.
- **Title:** *Vanna Venturi House, Scheme VI (final), scale model*.
- **Date:** 1959–1964; the house was built 1962–1964.
- **Institution:** MoMA, New York.
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/990).

**What it demonstrates:** The model represents a house with familiar components whose sizes and symmetry are deliberately unsettled. It demonstrates complexity within an apparently simple domestic outline. Distinguish the museum's cardboard-and-paper model from the building it represents.

**Image credit and reuse:** MoMA, object 132.1988; Gift of Venturi, Rauch and Scott Brown, Inc. Record copyright: Robert Venturi. Images are linked; reproduction follows museum licensing.

### Example 2: Portland Building

- **Creator:** Michael Graves.
- **Title:** *Portland Building*.
- **Date:** 1982; later reconstructed and reopened in 2020.
- **Institution:** City of Portland, Oregon (building owner and official documentation).
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://www.portland.gov/fleet-and-facilities/facilities/portland-building).

**What it demonstrates:** Its conspicuous facade treatment makes a civic building visually symbolic rather than anonymous. The official history identifies it as postmodern and also records practical problems that led to reconstruction. This separates historical significance from a claim that every aspect of the building worked well.

**Image credit and reuse:** Photographs and documentation: City of Portland; check individual image credits before reuse. Current images may show the reconstruction rather than the original 1982 condition.
