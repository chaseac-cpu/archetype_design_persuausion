# Deconstructive and Experimental Graphics

[Home](../README.md)

## What is it?

This page groups postmodern graphic approaches that question a single, transparent reading of a message. Katherine McCoy's work provides a documented deconstructive example; Ed Fella provides a related example of experimental, personally expressive lettering. These are connected approaches, not a claim that both designers belonged to one formal school. Layout, wording, and imagery can make interpretation part of the experience. [Historical context](https://cranbrookartmuseum.org/artwork/katherine-mccoy-posters-the-graduate-program-in-design/).

## How do you recognize or use it?

- Words may function simultaneously as language and visual shapes.
- Fragmentation or multiple reading routes can create ambiguity.
- Handmade lettering and unexpected combinations question uniformity.
- The viewer may need to interpret relationships rather than follow one obvious sequence.

**Suggested application:** A hypothetical literary journal could use a fragmented cover composition to introduce an issue about memory. Keep article text and essential navigation in a clear order. This approach can encourage interpretation in cultural work, but ambiguity is usually unhelpful in a payment form or essential instruction.

## Examples

These are historical works. Open each institutional link to view the work. Images are linked rather than reproduced because an open reuse license has not been established. ### Example 1: The Graduate Program in Design

- **Creator:** Katherine McCoy; printed by Signet Printing.
- **Title:** *The Graduate Program in Design*.
- **Date:** 1989.
- **Institution:** Cranbrook Art Museum, Bloomfield Hills, Michigan.
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://cranbrookartmuseum.org/artwork/katherine-mccoy-posters-the-graduate-program-in-design/).

**What it demonstrates:** Opposed words spread from a central axis while imagery and text complicate each other's roles. Cranbrook explicitly connects the composition with deconstructivist ideas and identifies it as postmodern graphic design. Its organization is conceptually motivated rather than simply disorderly.

**Image credit and reuse:** Cranbrook Art Museum, CAM 2008.97; Gift of Katherine and Michael McCoy. Image linked from the museum record; no reuse permission is assumed.

### Example 2: Flyer for lecture at Central Michigan University

- **Creator:** Edward (Ed) Fella.
- **Title:** *Flyer for lecture at Central Michigan University*.
- **Date:** 1995.
- **Institution:** LACMA, Los Angeles.
- **Direct source and image:** [View the work and institutional record](https://www.lacma.org/zap/node/35760).

**What it demonstrates:** LACMA discusses Fella's flyers as opportunities to explore self-expression, historic lettering, and everyday visual language. This example supports the experimental half of this page's category. It should not be treated as proof that every Fella work is formally deconstructivist.

**Image credit and reuse:** LACMA's Free Work in Due Time exhibition page credits © Ed Fella; photo © Museum Associates/LACMA. The museum's image is linked, not reproduced.
