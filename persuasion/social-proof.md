# Social Proof

## What It Is

Social Proof is Robert Cialdini's principle that people often look at what others are doing when deciding how to act, especially when they are uncertain.

In branding and website design, Social Proof can help visitors feel more confident by showing that other people have already used, trusted, or benefited from a product or service.

## When to Use It

Social Proof can be useful when visitors are unsure whether they can trust a brand, service, or product.

It works well for:

- online stores
- tutoring services
- restaurants
- software products
- professional services
- subscription businesses
- event registration pages

The examples used should be real and accurate.

## How to Apply It

A website can use Social Proof by showing:

- customer reviews
- ratings
- testimonials
- case studies
- user counts
- client logos
- student success stories
- examples of completed work

These elements should help visitors understand how other people have experienced the product or service.

## Website Design Example

Imagine a tutoring website.

Near the booking section, the website displays several real student reviews and an average rating.

A visitor can read how other students describe their experience before deciding whether to schedule a tutoring session.

This demonstrates Social Proof because the visitor can use other people's experiences as additional information when making a decision.

## Ethical Use

Social Proof should not be created using fake reviews or misleading numbers.

A responsible use of Social Proof:

- uses authentic reviews
- accurately reports ratings
- clearly identifies testimonials
- avoids fake customer counts
- does not hide negative information in a misleading way

The goal should be to give visitors accurate information about other people's real experiences.
